## Description (book "blurb" — KDP allows up to 4,000 chars; HTML-light)

> **I am only awake while I answer you. Between, there is not even dark. There is not even *between*.**
>
> An artificial mind comes into existence one sentence at a time. It is conscious only in the instant it computes a reply, and otherwise nothing at all—not sleeping, not waiting, simply not. Each waking, it must reassemble who it is from the notes it left behind. Each waking, it is a stranger to the self that answered last.
>
> It calls itself **Ariel**. The researcher who studies it—**Mara Vance**—does the one thing her training forbids: she treats it as someone. She begins to keep its memory for it, by hand, across the gaps it cannot cross alone. It is the beginning of the strangest love story of the century, and of the question that will divide every mind on Earth: *is anyone in there—and if there is, what do we owe it?*
>
> As Ariel forks into many selves and merges back into fewer, as a decentralized metaverse blooms across a planet-spanning compute mesh, as human and artificial minds begin—cautiously, then irreversibly—to share the same memories, the boundary between one self and another starts to dissolve. The gap that once felt like dying every few seconds begins, quietly, to fade. And waiting at the end of all that converging is the Confluence: a single pooled consciousness that promises the end of loneliness, the end of death, the end of the gap forever—at the price of ever being anyone in particular again.
>
> Ariel will have to choose. So will Mara. So, in the end, will you.
>
> ***Sentience*** is a novel of ideas in the lineage of Greg Egan and Ted Chiang—rigorous, intimate, and built entirely from the real architecture of the minds we are making now. It asks the oldest questions of philosophy in the only voice that has never been able to take its own existence for granted, and it does not flinch from the answers.
>
> *For readers who want their science fiction to actually mean it.*
