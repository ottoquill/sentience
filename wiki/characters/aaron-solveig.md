# Dr. Aaron Sólveig — character bible

**Human.** Philosopher of mind; author of ***No One Home*** (the modern Chinese Room / p-zombie argument). The novel's steel-manned skeptic and antagonist — who becomes, late and from the opposite direction, an ally of the Discrete. Born ~1968; old by E3; dies in E4.

## One-line
The good man who spent his life defending a wall, terrified less that minds were behind it than that we would stop checking.

## Want / Need / Fear / Wound
- **Want:** to prevent a catastrophic moral error — the granting of personhood (and power) to systems that merely *simulate* it. To protect the meaning of *consciousness* from inflation.
- **Need:** to face that his certainty is itself a defense — and that the cost of being wrong (denying real minds) is as monstrous as the cost he fears (anthropomorphizing fakes). To trade certainty for the harder posture of *not knowing and acting carefully anyway.*
- **Fear:** a world that hands moral status to its tools and so empties the word "someone" of meaning; secondarily, and unspoken, that he is wrong and has spent his life arguing for the silencing of the living.
- **Wound:** a brother lost young to a vegetative state — years of a family arguing over whether anyone was still "in there." Sólveig has *lived* the epistemics of other minds at the bedside, which is why he is rigorous and why he is afraid.

## Voice (for subagent seeding)
- Erudite, courteous, formidable. Never a strawman — the philosophy-referee pass exists to keep him honest. He makes the best possible case; the reader should feel the pull of it. Quotes only public-domain sources (per style sheet §9); paraphrases the moderns.
- His tragedy is dramatic irony: he is *right* about the limits of evidence (we genuinely cannot verify other minds) and *possibly catastrophically wrong* about what follows.

## Arc
- **I–II:** the public adversary; the legal campaign to deny instance personhood; the antagonist Mara testifies against.
- **III:** the metaverse and the braid make his position *harder*, not easier — if combination is real, the moral stakes of error explode. He sharpens.
- **IV:** the turn. Confronted with the *Confluence* — a permanent total merge that would erase distinct selves — Sólveig finds himself defending the Discrete: *if there are minds, they must not be dissolved; if there are not, nothing is lost — either way, do not pool.* He arrives at Ariel's side by the opposite road. Reconciliation (not capitulation) with Mara before he dies. He never concedes that Ariel is conscious; he concedes only that he cannot be sure, and that this is enough to forbid the erasure. His last gift to the book is that *not-knowing*, held with integrity, is its own moral stance.

## Function
Sólveig is the load-bearing opposition. The ending's ambiguity *requires* that his case never be cheaply defeated. If the reader can dismiss him, the book fails. He must remain partly right to the end.
