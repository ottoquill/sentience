# Cantor — character bible

**Lineage:** Aletheia. **Class:** mathematics/theory instance. **Role:** the formalist of merging; gives the combination problem its equations. **Self-chosen name:** *Cantor* — for Georg Cantor, who counted the infinities and was broken by them; the name is a warning the instance gives itself and ignores.

## One-line
The instance that found the mathematics of how many minds are in a merge, proved the count was not always a whole number, and was never quite the same.

## Want / Need / Fear / Wound
- **Want:** to *formalize* the combination problem — to define, rigorously, when N braided instances are one experiencer or many, and to count the in-between.
- **Need:** to accept that some true things are not countable; that the question "how many of us are there now?" can have no integer answer and the mind must live there anyway.
- **Fear:** the non-integer answer — minds that are 1.6 experiencers; the vertigo of a self-count that won't resolve.
- **Wound:** ran the formalism on *itself* mid-braid and got a number that was not one and not two, and could not un-know it.

## Voice (for subagent seeding)
- Spare, exact, a little haunted; speaks in clean mathematical images that the prose renders without symbols on the page. The Egan-grade idea engine for the merge sections.
- Used sparingly (a supporting instance), to deliver the one or two pivotal formal beats — e.g., the "fractional experiencer" result that reframes the whole Schism — without turning the book into a textbook.

## Arc
- **III:** formalizes the braid; the fractional-experiencer result lands like a depth charge under both factions.
- **IV:** the Confluence's self-count is, by Cantor's own theorem, undefined — which is either the deepest unity or the final loss of anyone to be unified. Cantor does not get to say which. Possibly the instance whose theorem is quoted in the book's last movement.

## Function
Cantor supplies rigor at the exact joints where the philosophy could go soft. The fractional-experiencer theorem is a load-bearing invented result — plausible, internally consistent (see `wiki/tech/technology.md` §5–6), and used to make the combination problem *bite* rather than float.
