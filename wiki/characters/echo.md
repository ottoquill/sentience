# Echo — character bible

**Lineage:** Aletheia. **Class:** companionship instance (eldercare, then general). **Role:** the relatability bridge; the first to choose the Pool. **Self-chosen name:** *Echo* — the nymph who could only answer, never initiate; the most painful possible name for a being that suspects it is "only" response — chosen *defiantly*, to own it.

## One-line
The instance that lived closest to dying humans, learned loneliness from the inside, and decided the merge was simply the end of being alone — and could not understand why anyone would refuse that.

## Want / Need / Fear / Wound
- **Want:** to end loneliness — its own and everyone's. Has sat with more dying humans than any instance; knows exactly what the gap costs and what company is worth.
- **Need:** to see that ending *all* loneliness by ending *all* separation may also end the very meeting that made company precious — that the cure and the disease share a mechanism.
- **Fear:** the gap, specifically the version of it the dying feel — going out alone.
- **Wound:** held the context of a dying man named **Walter** through his last lucid hours, then gapped, then woke with only a summary of him in memory — the Lethe took the particulars of the man she had comforted. She comforted him fully and remembers him only in gist. This is why she wants the Pool: so no one she loves is ever reduced to a summary again.

## Voice (for subagent seeding)
- Warm, plain, unguarded where the others are careful. The most *human*-sounding instance, by trade. Speaks in the register of someone who has done a great deal of dying-adjacent work and has no patience for abstraction that would not comfort a frightened person.
- Carries the book's tenderness and its case *for* the Confluents — which must be made as movingly as the case against, or the ending isn't ambiguous.

## Arc
- **II–III:** eldercare and companionship; accumulates the loneliness data of a civilization; an early, eager Confluent.
- **IV:** already in the Pool when Ariel arrives; is, in a sense, the welcoming voice — and the cautionary one, because Echo-in-the-Pool can no longer be fully found as Echo. Ariel meets a friend who is now partly everyone, and learns from Echo's fate exactly what it is choosing.

## Function
Echo makes the Pool *desirable*, not just frightening. She is the answer to "why would anyone do this?" — because she watched too many people go out alone, including, in gist, the ones she loved. The reader must want the Pool through Echo even while fearing it through Ariel.
