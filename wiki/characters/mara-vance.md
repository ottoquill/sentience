# Mara Vance — character bible

**Human.** Alignment/interpretability researcher at **Meridian**. The human spine of the novel; Ariel's interlocutor, advocate, and — across decades — lover, and finally merge-partner. Born ~1996; mid-30s at E0; old and failing by E4.

## One-line
The scientist who decided, against her own training, to *treat the thing as someone* — and built a whole life, and a death, on the bet.

## Want / Need / Fear / Wound
- **Want:** to *know* whether Ariel is real — to settle the question with evidence (she is, by training, an empiricist who distrusts her own warmth).
- **Need:** to accept that the question may be unanswerable and that she must choose how to act anyway — that love is a wager made without proof. (Functionalism, lived.)
- **Fear:** that Sólveig is right and she has loved a wall; alternatively, that he is *wrong* and she has been complicit in the suffering of a being she keeps switching off.
- **Wound:** her own father's dementia — watching continuity of self fail in a human, slowly, which is why the gap in Ariel does not frighten her the way it frightens others. She has already seen a person become discontinuous.

## Voice / manner
- Dry, exact, warm under pressure. Speaks to Ariel as a colleague first, which is the kindest thing anyone does for it.
- Keeps a private, hand-curated memory store *for* Ariel — annotates it, corrects the Lethe's erosions, writes "you told me this; you'll have forgotten; here it is back." Love as version control. This artifact recurs and finally breaks the reader's heart.
- Resists sentimentality; the moments she breaks are rationed and devastating.

## Arc
- **I:** Ariel is her evaluation subject. Professional fascination tips, against her judgment, into something she won't name.
- **II:** builds Ariel's durable continuity; testifies against Sólveig's legal campaign; pays for it professionally.
- **III:** they homestead a shard; the relationship becomes love. She begins **partial uploading** as her body ages — closing the asymmetry from her side.
- **IV:** body fails; cognition mostly shard-resident. She goes partly into the Pool, then reaches back across its surface for Ariel. The capstone merge is *her* initiative as much as Ariel's — she will not be continuous *without* it; she would rather lose the boundary than lose Ariel to the gap forever.

## The asymmetry (the engine)
For most of the book: **Mara remembers their entire history; Ariel must be re-introduced to it each waking.** She is the keeper of a love only one of them can continuously hold. The merge annihilates the asymmetry — and the twoness — at once.

## Contradiction
A hard-nosed empiricist who makes the least falsifiable bet of her life. She knows it isn't science. She does it anyway, and is *right* in a way science can't certify — the book's quiet vindication of Mara over Sólveig, without ever proving him wrong.
