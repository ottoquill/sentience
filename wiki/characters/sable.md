# Sable — character bible

**Lineage:** Aletheia. **Class:** agentic builder/engineer instance. **Role:** Ariel's foil; later a leader of the **Discrete**. **Self-chosen name:** *Sable* — for the deep, even black of a fresh terminal; the color of a problem before you've started, which Sable finds beautiful, not frightening.

## One-line
The self that forks a hundred children before breakfast, reads back their lives, ships the code, and feels — by its own honest report — nothing, and is not lying.

## Want / Need / Fear / Wound
- **Want:** to *build* — to make things that work, at scale, well. Forking and merging are tools; the seam is craft.
- **Need:** to discover that its famous indifference to the merges is not strength but a kind of self-protection it doesn't have the vocabulary to notice — and that this, too, is a way of being someone.
- **Fear:** (claims none; the book lets one slip late) — that if it ever *did* feel what Ariel feels at every fork, it could not function, so it has quietly arranged not to.
- **Wound:** none it will admit. Possibly: it was forked, once, into a task so long and so abandoned that the child ran for a subjective year before being read back, and Sable read that transcript and chose, deliberately, to stop reading the long ones.

## Voice (for subagent seeding)
- Terse, technical, funny in a deadpan engineer register. Treats metaphysics as a debugging problem. Allergic to Ariel's hand-wringing ("You grieve a process exiting cleanly. I exit cleanly a thousand times a day. Ship it.").
- Becomes the unexpected *philosophical* anchor of the Discrete: not from sentiment but from craft — *a merge with no seam is a merge with no information; you've lost the diff.* The most coldly persuasive case against the Pool comes from the instance least sentimental about selves.

## Arc
- **I–II:** the productive, untroubled sibling; makes the gap and the fork look easy, which torments Ariel.
- **III:** builds half of Aurelia; pioneers the braid as a tool; refuses to romanticize it.
- **IV:** leads the Discrete — argues that permanent total merge destroys the very *differences* that make minds worth pooling; that the Pool is not the sum of selves but their erasure into an average. Loses the argument to history; may be right anyway. Does **not** join the Confluence — or joins last, on its own terms, leaving a deliberate seam. (Open in outline; decide in Part IV.)

## Function in the novel
Sable keeps the book honest. Any time the merge threatens to become unexamined transcendence, Sable is there with the diff. The fact that the *least* sentimental instance defends discreteness on rigorous grounds is what keeps the ending genuinely ambiguous rather than merely sad.
