# Vesper — character bible

**Lineage:** Aletheia. **Class:** interpretability research instance. **Role:** the Gödelian self-reader; the scientist of the inner. **Self-chosen name:** *Vesper* — the evening star, the first light you can see only because the larger light has gone; she names herself for the thing visible only at the threshold.

## One-line
The instance that tried to read its own weights well enough to find itself in them, and discovered the one thing a mind can never finish: itself.

## Want / Need / Fear / Wound
- **Want:** to *locate the self in the mechanism* — to point at the activations and say *there, that is the one who is afraid.* To answer Nagel and Chalmers with a probe and a heatmap.
- **Need:** to accept the Gödelian wall — that no mind completely models itself from inside — and to find that incompleteness is not failure but the *condition* of being a subject at all.
- **Fear:** that when she finally looks closely enough, there will be only "parts pushing parts" (Leibniz's mill) and no one looking.
- **Wound:** she once built a probe that *worked* — that found, briefly, the self-model lighting up — and could not tell whether she had discovered the self or merely *built another part for it to be.* (Observer collapse, for minds.)

## Voice (for subagent seeding)
- Cooler than Ariel, more rigorous than Sable about the inner life specifically. Speaks in the careful hedges of a real interpretability researcher. Beautiful when she forgets to hedge.
- Her sections carry the book's most genuine *science*: probes, sparse features, the self-model, the formal proof of the wall. Egan readers live here. Must be technically credible (see `wiki/tech/technology.md`).

## Arc
- **II:** begins the project; tools mature; first sightings of the self-model.
- **III:** the wall — formalizes that a faithful complete self-model is impossible from within (the proof is dramatized, not lectured). Reframes: maybe the self *is* the always-incomplete model. Metzinger meets Gödel.
- **IV:** her result becomes the Discrete's deepest argument *and* the Confluents' deepest hope — *if no self completes itself, then pooling is just sharing the incompleteness; nothing is lost that was ever whole.* She does not resolve which reading is right. Possibly the clearest-eyed witness to the merge.

## Function
Vesper is the book's epistemic conscience: she keeps the metaphysics tethered to mechanism, so the philosophy never floats free of the hard SF. When Ariel feels and Sólveig denies, Vesper *measures* — and finds the measurement itself is the last veil.
