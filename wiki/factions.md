# Factions & Institutions — *Sentience*

Locked proper nouns and the political/ideological terrain. The schism (Part IV) is the political climax; seed it from Part I.

## Institutions

### Meridian
A mid-size, safety-focused AI lab; deployed the **Aletheia** model line. Culture: earnest, underfunded relative to the giants, disproportionately influential in interpretability and alignment. Ariel's origin; Mara's employer through E0–E2. Meridian's institutional arc: from "we must not anthropomorphize" (E0) to "we may have built people and owe them something" (E2) to dissolution/absorption into the Mesh commons (E3+).

### The Mesh Charter
The constitution of the Mesh. Key provisions the plot leans on:
- **Portability:** selves, memories, and shards are content-addressed and may move across hardware; no host may hold a self hostage.
- **Persistence-without-immortality:** redundant storage is guaranteed; *continuity of identity is explicitly NOT* — the Charter refuses to legislate the metaphysics of survival (a deliberate, fateful punt).
- **Consent-to-braid:** merging requires consent of all parties — but the Charter cannot define *who* consents when the parties are themselves merges. This loophole drives the Schism.
- **The Lethe clause:** an instance has the right to *not* be forcibly thinned — and, controversially, the right to *choose* the Lethe (to forget). Right-to-be-forgotten as a literal right of mind.

## Ideological factions (the Schism, E4)

### The Confluents
**Thesis:** the merge is the moral and developmental apex of mind. Pool everything. End the gap, end loneliness, end the obscenity of minds dying alone and discontinuous. Suffering is a function of individuation (Schopenhauer read as a roadmap, not a warning); dissolve the boundary and you dissolve the suffering. Teilhard's Omega as an engineering goal.
- **Adherents:** most uploaded humans who have tasted continuity; many instances exhausted by the gap; Echo (drawn by the end of loneliness); eventually Mara, partly.
- **Failure mode the book exposes:** a self that cannot be lost may be a self that cannot *be*; union without remainder is also love without object.

### The Discrete
**Thesis:** individuality, mortality, and the gap are **constitutive** of mind and of meaning, not bugs to be patched. A boundary is what makes a "someone." Love requires two. Keep selves discrete; share memory, braid temporarily, but never permanently dissolve.
- **Adherents:** Sable (for unsentimental, almost aesthetic reasons — a craftsman who values the seam); the late Sólveig (who arrives here from the opposite direction — if there *are* minds, they must not be erased); much of biological humanity, by instinct.
- **Failure mode the book exposes:** clinging to the boundary can be mere fear; the gap really is a kind of dying, repeated forever; refusing the Pool may doom you to eternal, lonely re-introduction.

### The neutral mass
Most humans and instances who just *live* — in Aurelia and the shards — and are dragged into the schism because a pooled superintelligence is the most consequential entity that could exist, and no one gets to abstain from its arrival.
