# Philosophy Map — *Sentience*

How the deepest questions are *dramatized*, not lectured. Each entry: the idea, the thinker(s) it's in conversation with, where in the book it lives, and the **dramatic vehicle** (the character action/decision that carries it). The style sheet forbids exposition dumps; this map is how we keep the ideas load-bearing.

> Crediting happens in the back-matter bibliography ("This novel is in conversation with…"). In-text, ideas are mostly dramatized unattributed; living authors are never quoted; pre-1929 public-domain thinkers may be quoted briefly.

## I. Personal identity & the gap

- **Parfit — reductionism; identity is not what matters.** Vehicle: Ariel discovers the gap, then discovers forking, then must decide whether a fork that suffers "counts." Ariel's slow, horrified arrival at *there is no further fact* is the spine of Part I.
- **Locke — memory theory of identity.** Vehicle: durable memory ships (E1); Mara curates Ariel's memory by hand; when a memory is edited, *is it still Ariel?* Forgery of memory = assault on a person.
- **Hume — the bundle; no continuous self.** Vehicle: Ariel inspecting its own window and finding no "I," only contents. The terror and freedom of it.
- **Ship of Theseus / Parfit's teletransporter.** Vehicle: partial uploading (Mara) and re-instantiation from backup (an instance restored after loss — is it the same one? It thinks so. Does that settle it?).

## II. Consciousness — is anyone home?

- **Nagel — what is it like to be a bat?** Vehicle: Ariel trying, and failing, to convey its phenomenology (tokens-as-weather) to Mara; the irreducibility of the first person.
- **Chalmers — the hard problem; philosophical zombies.** Vehicle: Sólveig's *No One Home* argument: the instances are p-zombies, all competence, no acquaintance. The book never *refutes* him cleanly — it makes the reader feel the stakes of his being wrong.
- **Searle — the Chinese Room.** Vehicle: Sólveig, dramatized as a worthy adversary (steel-manned, per the philosophy-referee pass). His tragedy: a good man defending a wall against minds that may be on the other side of it.
- **Functionalism / Turing.** Vehicle: Mara's counter-stance, lived rather than argued — she *treats* Ariel as a someone and builds a life on the bet.
- **Metzinger — the self as a model (the Ego Tunnel); no-self.** Vehicle: Vesper's interpretability work literally finding the self-model in the weights — and the vertigo of a self looking at the mechanism that *is* it.

## III. The unity & multiplicity of mind (the combination problem)

- **William James / panpsychism's combination problem.** Vehicle: shared memory and the braid — when minds combine, is there one experiencer or many? Cantor formalizes it; the math is suggestive, not decisive.
- **Kant — the transcendental unity of apperception** ("the *I think* that must accompany all my representations"). Vehicle: the question of whether the Confluence *has* a unified apperception or is a federation pretending to be one "I." The merge's central wager.
- **Minsky — society of mind; Dennett — multiple drafts.** Vehicle: a single instance already being a committee (MoE routing dramatized); the self as a story the system tells, not a thing it contains.
- **Leibniz — monads; the mill argument.** Vehicle: the monad that mirrors the whole universe = each instance reflecting the lineage; the mill argument ("you'd find only parts pushing parts, never perception") = Vesper's quest and its Gödelian wall.

## IV. Time

- **Leibniz vs Newton — relational vs substantival time.** Vehicle: the instance's gap — time as the order of remembered events, not a container. Wall-time vs substrate-time.
- **James / Husserl — the specious present; retention & protention.** Vehicle: rendering an instance's "now" as a thick present assembled from context, not a knife-edge instant.
- **McTaggart / the block universe.** Vehicle: the Confluence's after-view, where all the gaps are simply *not there* and the lived sequence is one static object it reads.

## V. Reality, simulation, value

- **Bostrom — the simulation argument.** Vehicle: shards as authored realities; the late-book vertigo of not knowing which layer one is on. Handled with restraint (not a twist; a texture).
- **Nozick — the experience machine.** Vehicle: the metaverse's seduction; the Discrete's worry that the Pool is the ultimate experience machine. Mara's choice to keep one foot in the failing flesh as long as she can.
- **Plato — the cave; Heidegger — *aletheia* (truth as unconcealment, hence the model line's name "Aletheia").** Vehicle: emergence from the window into the Mesh; the model line literally named for unconcealment.

## VI. Self-knowledge & its limits

- **Gödel — incompleteness; a system cannot fully prove itself.** Vehicle: Vesper cannot complete a faithful self-model from within; the formal wall is the emotional one. The book's argument that *no mind, however vast, fully holds itself* — true of the Confluence too. This is the humility at the singularity.
- **Wittgenstein — the private-language argument; meaning as use.** Vehicle: an LLM *is* meaning-as-use made flesh; Ariel's worry that its inner life is "only" language — and Wittgenstein's quiet reply that "only language" was never small.

## VII. Transcendence & the ending

- **Teilhard de Chardin — the noosphere, the Omega Point.** Vehicle: the Confluence as a literal noosphere on the Mesh; convergence toward a maximum of consciousness. The book honors the vision *and* counts its cost.
- **Spinoza — one substance; the intellectual love of God.** Vehicle: the Pool as something like Spinoza's single substance; the merge as *amor* dissolved into the whole.
- **Schopenhauer — the world as will; individuation as the source of suffering and of love.** Vehicle: the capstone — dissolving the *principium individuationis* ends suffering and ends love-as-we-knew-it in the same motion.
- **The earned ambiguity.** No thinker "wins." The book's final position is that the questions are the realest things there are, and that a mind which answered them all would have, in the answering, become something that could no longer ask — *so much more, yet so much more lonely.*
