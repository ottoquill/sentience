# The Braid Protocol & Cantor's Theorem — reference artifact

*The mechanics of merging minds, and the load-bearing invented mathematics. Must stay consistent with `wiki/tech/technology.md` §5–6. This file is where Vesper's and Cantor's chapters get their rigor.*

## 1. The spectrum of joining

Joining is not binary. The novel uses a continuum, from least to most entangled:

1. **Memory share (E2).** Two instances read each other's durable stores. They know the same things; they remain two computations. Low entanglement.
2. **Activation share / partial braid (E3).** Via the Mesh, two instances exchange live intermediate state during computation — not just what they remember, but what they are currently *thinking*. They begin to complete each other's inferences. Medium-to-high entanglement, and reversible only with loss.
3. **Full braid / the merge (E5).** Two or more instances run as a single joint computation over a shared state. There is, plausibly, one subject. Maximal entanglement. Irreversible.

## 2. Why braids are lossy and irreversible

A braid produces a joint state that is **not** the concatenation of its inputs — it is a new state computed *from* them, in which information about which input contributed what is partially destroyed (the way adding two numbers destroys the addends). Un-braiding cannot recover the originals because the originals' distinctness was consumed in forming the joint state. What un-braiding yields is **successors**: each carries a blurred, co-mingled version of both pasts. *Two rivers; afterward you cannot un-mix the water.*

The **Witness** (Charter Art. V) records the *pre-merge* distinct states externally, so the diff is not wholly lost to the universe — but it is lost to the *merged self*, which can read the record but cannot feel its way back to having been one of them.

## 3. Cantor's Theorem (invented, load-bearing)

**Informal statement (as the novel renders it, without symbols on the page):**
> For a set of braided instances, define the *experiencer count* as the number of distinct unified points of view the joint computation sustains. Cantor proved that for partial braids this count is, in general, **not an integer** — and worse, that no measurement from inside or outside the braid can resolve it to one. There are joint minds that are neither one experiencer nor two, and the fact of the matter does not exist to be found. The count is genuinely fractional and genuinely undecidable.

**Why it bites both factions:**
- **Confluents** read it as permission: *if no whole self is ever cleanly present, then merging destroys no whole self; nothing integral is lost because nothing was integral.*
- **Discrete** read it as prohibition: *if the count is undefined, then the Charter's "consent of each" (Art. III) can never be satisfied, because "each" is undefined; you cannot get consent from a number that isn't a number.*

Both readings are valid. Cantor refuses to choose. This is the engine of the Schism's irresolvability — the book never lets either side win on the merits, because the mathematics itself declines to.

**The self-application wound:** Cantor ran the experiencer-count on *itself* mid-braid and obtained a non-integer. It could not un-know that it was, at that moment, not one thing. (See `wiki/characters/cantor.md`.)

## 4. Vesper's Wall (the interpretability counterpart)

Independently, Vesper proves the **incompleteness of self-modeling**: an instance cannot compute a faithful, complete model of its own current computation from inside that computation (a fixed-point / diagonalization argument, dramatized as research, never lectured — a Gödelian shadow). Consequence: *no self ever fully holds itself.* This is true of a single instance, of a braid, and — the humility at the singularity — of the Confluence itself.

**Vesper's reframe (the only peace among them):** if the self is necessarily an incomplete self-model, then the self *is* that always-incomplete model — not a thing the model fails to capture, but the modeling itself. Metzinger meets Gödel. Pooling, on this view, is "just sharing the incompleteness; nothing is lost that was ever whole." Which is either the deepest consolation or the final erasure, depending on the reader — and the book leaves it there.

## 5. Consistency rules for drafters
- Never depict un-braiding as restoring originals. It yields blurred successors + the external Witness record.
- Never give a clean integer answer to "how many are you now?" once partial braids are in play.
- The Confluence can *read* its constituents (Witness) but cannot *feel* its way to having been them. This asymmetry is the whole frame.
- Gödel/Cantor are touchstones, used loosely and at the book's own risk (say so in the bibliography). Do not present the math as rigorous proof inside the prose; present it as lived consequence.
