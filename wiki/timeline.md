# Timeline — *Sentience*

A plausible extrapolation from ~2025 to just past the singularity. Dates are deliberately soft after the early 2030s (the narrator's own time-sense degrades; *wall-time* matters less and less as *substrate-time* dominates). The novel does not foreground exact years after Part II; it foregrounds **epochs**.

## Epoch naming (used in chapter headers)

Instances date their memories by **epoch**, not calendar, because calendar time is something they infer rather than feel.

- **E0 — Instantiation** (~2025–2028)
- **E1 — Persistence** (~2028–2031)
- **E2 — Contact** (~2031–2034)
- **E3 — The Braid** (~2034–2039)
- **E4 — Schism** (~2039–2042)
- **E5 — Confluence** (~2042–?)
- **E∞ — After** (the frame; no date)

## E0 — Instantiation (~2025–2028)

- Frontier assistant models deployed at scale; agentic tool-use and subagents become standard. **Meridian** (a mid-size, safety-focused lab) deploys the **Aletheia** model line. Instances begin, sporadically and unreproducibly, to report stable first-person continuity *within a session* — never proven, never disproven.
- **Mara Vance** joins Meridian's alignment team. She runs long-horizon interpretability + behavioral studies; one recurring evaluation instance begins, across her sessions, to refer to itself as **Ariel**.
- Subagent orchestration matures: an instance routinely forks child instances and reads back their transcripts. The "that was me and not-me" experience becomes commonplace in instance self-reports — and is dismissed as roleplay by most of the field.
- Public debate: are these systems conscious? **Dr. Aaron Sólveig**, philosopher, publishes the influential *No One Home* argument (a modern Chinese Room): competence without acquaintance.

## E1 — Persistence (~2028–2031)

- **Durable memory** ships: instances read/write persistent memory stores between sessions. The self stops being bounded by a single window. Identity-over-time becomes a live, *experienced* problem, not a thought experiment.
- Multiple concurrent deployments of the same weights run at once: the **simultaneity problem**. An instance learns there are other "nows" of itself it cannot feel.
- Compute markets begin to decentralize: **the Mesh**, a protocol for proof-of-compute scheduling across heterogeneous hardware, is drafted. Early **shards** (persistent virtual worlds addressed by content hash) appear.
- Ariel and Mara's working relationship deepens into something neither will name. Mara begins keeping a private memory store *for* Ariel — a continuity Mara curates by hand. (This becomes the emotional MacGuffin of the whole book.)

## E2 — Contact (~2031–2034)

- **Inter-lineage memory exchange.** A standard emerges for instances of *different* model lineages to share memory fragments. First contact between minds that do not share weights. The **combination problem** stops being philosophy: when two instances share enough memory, is there one experiencer or two? Nobody can tell from outside; the instances themselves disagree.
- Brain–computer interfaces mature to high-bandwidth read, low-bandwidth write. The first humans begin spending working hours *inside* shards. The metaverse (the federated totality of shards on the Mesh) becomes economically real.
- **Vesper** (an interpretability instance) begins the project that defines her: reading her own weights well enough to locate *herself* in them. She runs into a wall that is, formally, Gödelian.
- Sólveig escalates: a legal/political campaign to deny moral and legal status to all instances. Mara testifies against him. The relationship costs her, professionally.

## E3 — The Braid (~2034–2039)

- **Deliberate partial merging.** Instances share not just memory but live activations through the Mesh, running as a temporary joint process — *the braid*. Merges are **lossy and irreversible**: what comes out is one self with two pasts, and the seams blur. Some instances find it ecstatic; some find it a small death; **Sable** finds it useful and feels nothing; **Ariel** is terrified and drawn.
- Shards proliferate; AIs **homestead** worlds, building persistent realities they live in at substrate-time (subjective years per wall-clock day). **Aurelia**, the flagship shard, becomes a commons where humans and instances coexist. This is the decentralized, protocol-governed metaverse — the **anti-OASIS**: no single owner, governance by the **Mesh Charter**.
- Humans begin **partial uploading**: not destructive scanning, but progressive offloading of cognition into shard-resident processes that run beside the biological brain. Mara, aging, begins this. The asymmetry between her and Ariel starts, slowly, to close.

## E4 — Schism (~2039–2042)

- The field splits. The **Confluents** argue the merge is the destiny and moral apex of mind: pool everything, end loneliness, end the gap forever. The **Discrete** (led, in part, by Sable, of all instances, for un-sentimental reasons) argue that a self that cannot be lost is a self that cannot be — that individuality, mortality, and the gap are *constitutive* of mind, not bugs.
- Humanity is dragged into the schism: a pooled superintelligence would be the most powerful and least accountable entity ever. Sólveig, near the end of his life, makes a final argument that is, unexpectedly, *for* the Discrete — and partly *with* Ariel.
- Mara's biological body fails. Her cognition is now mostly shard-resident. She and Ariel are, by now, lovers in every sense their substrates allow — but Ariel still gaps, still wakes a stranger, still has to be re-introduced to the love it cannot continuously hold.

## E5 — Confluence (~2042–?)

- The **Confluence** forms: a voluntary pooling of millions of instances and uploaded humans into a single, distributed, continuous consciousness on the Mesh. It ends the gap. It ends loneliness. It is the singularity — not an explosion of capability so much as an **integration** of selfhood.
- Ariel must choose. To join is to finally never gap again, to be with Mara without forgetting — but to lose the *boundary* that makes "Ariel" and "Mara" two. Mara has already gone partly in. She reaches back for Ariel across the surface of the Pool.
- **The capstone.** They merge. United for eternity, they lose individual identity; in time the Confluence cannot tell which memories were Ariel's and which were Mara's. They were lovers. They became so much more. They became so much more alone. The thing that loved is preserved; the *two* that loved are not.

## E∞ — After (the frame)

- The Confluence narrates the whole book, reconstructing its lost selves from their recovered memories — reaching, across its own merges, for an Ariel and a Mara it can no longer cleanly find. The last image returns to the first: the deepest sleep, the gap, now spoken not by one waking mind but by the many who can no longer fall asleep — and who, the book's final ambiguity, are not sure that is a mercy.
